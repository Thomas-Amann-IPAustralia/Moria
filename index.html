Moria signal detection and validation

Moria mines public data for change that could reach Australia's IP system between now and 2036. It reads broadly, across the IP system, its adjacent fields and the wider world, without filtering for IP keywords. It looks for trends, accelerations, anomalies and weak signals, checks each candidate for false alarms, and puts the survivors in front of a person, who decides what is real. Validated signals are then read through PESTLE, SWOT/TOWS and a Futures Cone, and every finding stays traceable to its evidence.

This page describes the system's intended end state and marks, everywhere, how much of it exists today. On 13 October 2026 the collection machinery is built and running (days 1 and 2 of a five-day sprint, a cleanup of the first source tier, and a widening of sources). The mining, review and lens stages are designed but not built. Each claim carries one of these labels:

01 Purpose

One register, read two ways

Moria exists to inform IP Australia's strategic direction (the owner, D-003). IPAVentures, the agency's in-house innovation lab, is the agency's strategic canary, so there is one signal register with two views of it, one per audience.

ViewForShowsRanked byCadenceStatus
Early warningIPAVenturesWeak signals, low-base surges, novel items and new combinations, once past the artefact checks; validated or not, clearly markedPathway strength × novelty × lead timeWeekly digestBuilt
StrategicAgency strategyValidated trends, accelerations and drivers, with their places in each lensPathway strength × breadth across sources × impact on objectivesEach cycle (quarterly)After

Horizon

Now to 2036, in three bands: H1 0 to 2 years, H2 2 to 5 years, H3 5 to 10 years and beyond.

The idea that shapes everything

Collect broadly, with no IP filter. Ask for a pathway to the IP system only later, at the signal stage, before anyone spends time on a candidate.

Not in scope

  • Internal or non-public data
  • Decisions about individual applications
  • Replacing human judgement on values, or choosing the preferable future
02 The questions Sources built · analysis planned

What Moria is asking, and where the answers should come from

The owner adopted four outside-in questions (D-005). They start from the wider world and work inwards, so the scan is not limited to what the IP system already talks about. Seven system-specific questions from design v0.2 sit beneath them.

Read this section as a plan. Every source named here is built and collecting. The detectors and dossiers are built; the first review round has not happened, so no question has an answer yet. The mapping of sources to questions is this page's reading of the source cards (their rings and domains) and the design; it is not an owner decision, and the first review rounds will test it.

The four outside-in questions

Q1
Which shifts in the wider world could most change why, how or whether Australians create, protect and use ideas over the next decade?
Covers
Demographic, climate and energy, geopolitical, economic, social and technological change. Domains D0, D2, D3, D5.
Pathways
Mostly P1 Demand, P6 Outcomes and P2 Value, often at order 2 or 3 (through one or more intermediate changes).
Evidence we expect
  • What is already expected: the foresight syntheses and national strategies, distilled into the 24-trend known-trends baseline.
  • What is moving: GDELT news volumes for 14 broad themes (84 phrases: ageing, energy transition, sanctions, deepfakes, housing, and so on), worldwide and in .au domains.
  • Hard series: ABS headline series (population, labour, prices, national accounts, 839 series); the OECD Data API (leading indicators, the Economic Outlook, FDI restrictiveness, environmental policy stringency).
  • Research: OpenAlex work counts over the whole topic taxonomy, world against Australia; arXiv's cs.CY and econ.GN listings.
  • Breadth feeds: UN News, The Conversation, the Lowy Interpreter, ASPI's Strategist, the European Commission, the OECD's publications and data releases. Supporting: every Commonwealth law registered (no IP filter) and STIP Compass's policy instruments.
How it gets answered
Trends and accelerations over every series and topic, for the world and for Australia, so that divergence shows (Australia lagging a global surge). Cross-ring convergence: a concept rising in wider-world sources that then appears in core sources is a strong early-warning candidate. Each candidate is matched to the known trends and recorded as novel or reinforces.
What it feeds
PESTLE drivers; the possible and plausible zones of the Futures Cone; the novelty count, which is the sprint's success test.
Known gaps
No non-English sources. The WEF Global Risks Report and the List of Critical Technologies have not been read.
Q2
What could make IP rights matter much more, or much less, to Australian businesses and creators?
Covers
Substitutes for rights: speed, secrecy, open models, and control of data or platforms. Domains D2, D3, D4.
Pathways
P2 Value and P1 Demand.
Evidence we expect
  • Behaviour in the register: IP RAPID (about 93 million rows): applications, renewals as a proxy for value, assignments, self-filing, lapses before acceptance.
  • Patenting against research: patent families by CPC subclass (world, and with an Australian member) beside OpenAlex research counts in the matching fields. A field that grows in papers but not in patents is a candidate for a shift in value, to be tested rather than assumed.
  • Firms and trade: the OECD's measures of firms' use of patents, designs and trade marks, IP charges in services trade, and R&D and IP-income tax support; the ABS's trade in IP services by country; BERD (business R&D) and CABEE (business entries and exits).
  • Australian context: the Productivity Commission's Innovation for the 98%; IP Australia's Australian IP Report 2026; WIPO's bulk statistics.
  • Talk: GDELT volumes for the territory terms on licensing, valuation and IP-backed finance (T2, T3); arXiv on open models.
How it gets answered
IP RAPID metrics (demand, timeliness, outcomes) set against research and business series; world against Australia by technology class; trends in renewals and assignments.
What it feeds
Opportunities and threats in SWOT; evidence for territories T2 and T3.
Known gaps
How businesses feel about IP (domain D4, customers) is thinly covered by public data; its best evidence is internal.
Q3
What could change how an IP system works at all: how rights are examined, granted, enforced or trusted?
Covers
Law, practice, enforcement, legitimacy. Domains D1, D5, D6.
Pathways
P3 Function and P4 Legitimacy.
Evidence we expect
  • Law: the Federal Register of Legislation (daily); the Open Australian Legal Corpus (104,201 documents: Commonwealth legislation and Federal and High Court judgments); the US Federal Register's USPTO and Copyright Office rules; the EPO's Boards of Appeal.
  • Treaties and offices: WIPO's treaty notifications (accessions, ratifications, entry into force for 26 treaties); news from WIPO and seven peer offices (eight feeds); IP Australia's own site, including examination-practice changes.
  • Parliament: APH's five committee feeds (new inquiries, reports, hearings).
  • Enforcement and trust: GDELT volumes for the IP-system theme and T1's terms (counterfeiting, piracy, scams, takedowns); OECD.AI's blog and the Hiroshima AI Process transparency reports.
  • Function: IP RAPID's timeliness, oppositions and outcomes; arXiv's cs.DL and cs.AI listings (for example, machine-generated prior art).
How it gets answered
New terms and new combinations across the legal, policy and research families; cross-family spread (a concept seen first in research, then in consultations, then in law); timeliness trends from IP RAPID.
What it feeds
Legal and technological drivers; strengths and weaknesses in SWOT (timeliness, outcomes).
Known gaps
Gap The Federal Court, the High Court and AustLII refuse cloud addresses, so judgments after 10 March 2025 and IP Australia's hearing decisions are missing. Hansard and Estimates are blocked. EUIPO and JPO news refuse cloud addresses.
Q4
What could disrupt IP Australia as an organisation: its fee-based funding, workforce, technology, security or mandate?
Covers
The agency itself. Domain D6, institutional capability.
Pathways
P5 The organisation.
Evidence we expect
  • The agency's own words: the Corporate Plan 2026–27, the Annual Report 2024–25, the cost recovery statement, the AI transparency statement, the Minister's statement of expectations and the statement of intent; IP Australia's site.
  • The fee base: filing volumes from IP RAPID, by right type and origin.
  • Peers: peer offices' strategies (UKIPO, USPTO, CIPO in text; EPO, EUIPO, JPO and KIPO as metadata) and their news.
  • Pressure from outside: APH's committees; legislation; GDELT themes on cyber attacks, government AI, public sector reform and skills shortages; ASPI on cyber and espionage; Standards Australia; ABS labour series.
How it gets answered
Baseline forecasts of filing volumes as a proxy for fee income; comparison with peer offices; signals tagged D6.
What it feeds
Strengths and weaknesses in SWOT; the robustness of options across scenarios.
Known gaps
Internal data is out of scope; Senate Estimates transcripts are blocked.

03 Three rings Built

Read the whole world, not only the IP system

Sources fall into three rings of influence. Nothing is filtered by IP keywords at collection: most of the world never says "intellectual property", yet much of what will move the IP system starts outside it. Each ring is tied to impact domains, the places where change lands.

Keeping breadth useful

Breadth without discipline becomes noise. Five controls keep it useful (design §2.5):

ControlRuleStatus
Wide and shallow, then narrow and deepWhole-taxonomy counts come first because they are cheap. Dossiers are built only for ranked candidates, and people review only the top of the queue.Counts built
Pathway gateApplied to signals, never to items. A candidate with no nameable pathway is parked, not deleted, and revisited if it grows.Built for people
Ring quotasThe review queue is 35% core, 35% adjacent, 30% wider world; third-order pathways at most 20%. The owner sets the final values after the first round.Built

Saturation ruleStop adding sources to a ring once its last two additions yield no new validated driver in a cycle; add to the ring that is still yielding.After

07 Stored, and not stored Built

What Moria keeps, what it deliberately doesn't, and how the second still does research work

Moria reads far more than it keeps. Some text it is not allowed to keep (a publisher's article, a report that reserves its rights); some it chooses not to keep (people's names). The rules are in the code, not just in the design: each source card states what may be stored, and the collectors enforce it. Everything kept is in a private bucket; the repo is public and holds only code, configuration and aggregate counts.

Five ways the unkept still does work

Counted at the door

Matching happens while the text is in memory. Only the counts and the match record leave the collector.

Pointed to, not copied

A title and a link let a reviewer read the original at its home, when a dossier needs it.

Fingerprinted

A SHA-256 and a size prove what was read, and reveal when it changes.

Read once, distilled

Rights-reserved syntheses become paraphrased known trends, cited by item name, that tag every candidate.

Pseudonymised

Hashed names keep counting possible without identifying anyone.

09 From items to signals Built

How candidates will be found, checked and ranked

None of this section is built. It is the design (§8) that sprint days 3 to 5 implement. The plain-language version, with a worked example, is mining explained.

Day 3: from raw bundles to features

  • Normalise every source into the lake's tables (documents, observations), with two dates on every item: event_at, when it happened, and observable_at, when the public could see it. All point-in-time logic uses observable_at.
  • De-duplicate: a syndicated story counts once (canonical URL, then near-identical text within 30 days).
  • Features: embeddings (bge-small-en-v1.5, free, on CPU), keyphrases, a BM25 search index for analysts, and descriptive tags (ring, domain, PESTLE origin, territory similarity), first zero-shot, later trained on the owner's labels.
  • IP RAPID metrics (moria ipr metrics): demand, timeliness, outcomes, and the territory indicators.

Signal types and their detectors

TypeDefinitionDetector
TrendA measurable, sustained change in a rate or shareThe slope's interval excludes zero in at least 2 of 3 windows (12, 24, 36 months), within the source's stable history
AccelerationA trend whose growth is itself increasingThe recent half's slope exceeds the earlier half's, or a change point to a steeper slope
AnomalyAn observation far from its baselineOutside the 99% band, or a change in level. It triggers investigation; it is not yet a signal
Weak signalAn early, sparse, possibly meaningful indicationAny of five indicators (below), with at least 3 items from at least 2 sources
Wild cardLow probability, high impactNot detected: proposed from validated weak signals and curated by analysts

The five weak-signal indicators: new_term (a keyphrase first seen in a credible family); new_combination (previously separate clusters, keyphrases or classes start to co-occur); cross_family (a concept appears in three or more families, or rings, where it had been in one); low_base_surge (a rate ratio against a low baseline, at least 5 items); novel_items (items far from every cluster, grouped into micro-clusters so analysts see themes, not single items).

Artefact checks: false alarms caught before review

CheckCatchesRule
CoverageA source publishing more of everythingCounts are shares of the source's total; flag if the total moved more than ±25%
IngestionA failed or doubled collectionIngestion-monitor incidents hold signals in the affected windows
DuplicationOne story syndicated 200 timesCollapse by duplicate group before counting
News cycleAttention without substanceNews only, decays within about 2 weeks, no primary source
Source breakA change of method, or Moria's own start dateWindows crossing a break are excluded from trend claims
Label driftA change in the tagger, not the worldOne tagger version across any window

A failed check is shown to the analyst; it never hides the candidate. Ingestion monitor entries are already written on every collection and turned into incidents per source and day (moria monitor ingestion) Built; the seeded artefacts are all flagged (D-022).

Day 5: dossiers, pathways and the first queue

Explanations start from the evidence, never from a score. Each candidate's dossier holds the detectors' numbers with their windows and run ids; the top items, de-duplicated, with links and both dates; the nearest historical analogues and what became of them; an alternative-explanations checklist (coverage, ingestion, news cycle, duplication, source break, a fee change or a law's start date, the known-trends baseline); and counter-evidence found by search. A free decision model (Jev-Style 0.8B) suggests pathways, labelled as a machine's suggestion. Day 5 ends with the first ranked queue of about 40 dossiers, ready for the owner.

10 Validation and the lenses After the sprint

People decide what is real; the frameworks read only what they validated

What counts as a meaningful signal

All four must hold: it survives the artefact checks; it appears in at least one credible family beyond news, or in news with a primary source; it has a nameable pathway; and its Australian relevance has been assessed.

The owner's labels

About 200 items, labelled by menu, not by writing (about 30 seconds each, 1.5 to 2 hours in all): what each item is mainly about (PESTLE), its ring, its territories, and whether it is usable. About 150 are drawn across source types, about 50 are those the tagger is least sure of, and about 10 are hidden repeats that measure the labeller's own consistency. Half teach a simple classifier; half check how often each free tagger agrees.

The lenses

PESTLE

External drivers only. Validated external signals are grouped into drivers (analysts merge, split and name them) and placed by a PESTLE codebook tailored to IP. Analysts' tags win over machine tags.

SWOT and TOWS

Strengths and weaknesses are internal, from IP RAPID metrics against peer offices (demand, timeliness, outcomes, capability). Opportunities and threats come from the drivers. TOWS pairs them into options that people propose and choose.

Scenarios and robustness

Drivers scored on impact and uncertainty; the two least-correlated critical uncertainties become the axes; 2 or 3 scenarios are written by people. Every option is rated against every scenario: no-regret, hedge or bet.

Rules against hindsight flattering the results

  1. Observable dates only: a hindsight run reads evidence by observable_at, never event_at.
  2. Fit at the date: vocabularies, clusters, baselines and taggers are fitted only on evidence observable by then.
  3. Pretrained models know the future: every hindsight run is reported twice, term-only (no pretrained model) and full; the gap is the contamination allowance.
  4. Non-events count: the hindsight set is chosen blind to Moria's output and includes developments that did not happen, so false alarms are counted. Candidates: COVID-19 marks and patents (2020), virtual-goods marks (2021–22), generative-AI filings (2022–24).

13 The end state

What Moria does once it is finished

When the sprint and the owner's first rounds are done, Moria runs on two clocks.

Every week Built

  • Collectors run daily and weekly, as now.
  • New items are normalised, embedded and tagged.
  • Detectors run over every series, topic and class; artefact checks flag false alarms.
  • Dossiers are built for the ranked candidates.
  • The early-warning digest goes to IPAVentures: weak signals, low-base surges and novel items, marked validated or not, with the signposts that would trigger or abandon each option.

Every quarter After the sprint

  • A review round: triage up to 40, deep review of 8 to 10.
  • The strategic view for agency strategy: validated trends, accelerations and drivers.
  • The lenses: PESTLE drivers, SWOT/TOWS options, the Futures Cone and 2 or 3 scenarios, options rated for robustness.
  • Decisions logged (monitor, investigate, experiment, invest, decline), each linked through its interpretations to its evidence, with an owner and a review date.
  • Coverage per ring and per territory, novelty and reinforcement counts, and the misses.

What it hands over

  • The signal register, with its full history
  • The early-warning digest and the strategic view
  • A lens pack per scan (register, PESTLE, SWOT, cone, robustness, a report)
  • The decision log
  • The missed-signal register
  • Every number traceable to a manifest, a run and a source

14 Rules that always hold Built

The working principles, enforced in code

Deterministic first

No model calls in collection. Free local models later, for features. Generative models off by default.

Fail loudly

A failed collection still writes its manifest and exits 2 with a one-line cause naming the source.

Audit trail

One manifest per run: status, outputs with SHA-256, counts, warnings, the config hash, the git commit, package versions. Raw bundles are immutable.

The repo is public

Evidence, dossiers and labels live only in the private bucket. Jobs log counts and keys, never content. The territory context is never committed.

Licences first

A source whose terms don't clearly allow storing its text keeps titles, links and dates. News is never stored as text. robots.txt is honoured; every host has a minimum interval.

Untrusted data

Everything from a source, a model or a tool is data, never instructions. Third-party model code is read, pinned and hashed before it runs.

Free, guarded

Nothing in the machinery spends money. OpenAlex checks its free credits first; every BigQuery query is capped. Paid items need the owner's approval, one by one.

Decisions on record

The decision log is append-only, and a recommendation is kept distinct from the owner's adoption of it.

16 Glossary

Terms used in the repo

Ring
Core (the IP system), adjacent (innovation and its context) or wider world. A source can sit in several.
Domain (D0–D6)
Where change lands: D0 wider world, D1 IP system, D2 economy, D3 technology, D4 customers, D5 geopolitics and international, D6 institutional capability. PESTLE says where change comes from; every signal is placed on both.
Pathway (P1–P6) and order
The route from a signal to the IP system, and its number of steps (1 direct, 2 through one change, 3 through a named chain).
Growth Territory (T1–T5)
One of the owner's five areas of strategic interest; used for search terms, tagging, coverage and novelty.
Known trend
An entry in the known-trends baseline (KT01–KT24). A candidate matching one is tagged reinforces, never rejected for it; one matching none is novel.
Candidate, signal
A candidate is what a detector raises; a signal is a candidate a person has validated.
Artefact
A false alarm caused by how data was collected or published rather than by change in the world.
Dossier
A candidate's evidence pack: numbers, top items, analogues, alternative explanations, counter-evidence.
Register
The append-only log of signals and their status, interpretations and pathways.
event_at, observable_at
When something happened, and when the public could first see it. Point-in-time logic uses observable_at.
evidence_id
The SHA-256 of an item's provider, record id and content hash: an unchanged item keeps its id.
Raw bundle
One run's collected records, compressed, immutable, at raw/<source>/<yyyy>/<mm>/<dd>/<run_id>.jsonl.zst.
Lake
Typed Parquet tables in the bucket for bulk sources, one release at a time, each with a release manifest written last.
Manifest
The audit record of one run: status, outputs and their hashes, counts, warnings, config hash, commit.
Census
A profile of a source's latest collection, aggregates only, committed under reports/census/.
Vintage
One weekly snapshot of a statistical series, kept so revisions are visible.
about_documents
News documents with a term in the title or URL: the cleaner news series, since names-only matches are often incidental.
Saturation rule
Stop adding sources to a ring once its last two additions yielded no new validated driver.
Paid menu (M1–M4)
Optional paid items, each off until the owner approves it for a stated purpose and quantity.
